## Digital rights initiative — policy guardrails
Goal: protect every American's ability to access, obtain, understand, correct, export and securely use their own personal information, including information held by public bodies and covered private entities, and protect lawful internet access from arbitrary retaliation.

A proposal cannot create blanket immunity from criminal investigation or prosecution. It should prohibit punishment solely for lawful access to one's own information or exercise of protected rights, while preserving constitutionally valid investigations and prosecutions based on independent evidence of unlawful conduct. Government access to private information remains subject to constitutional standards and applicable warrants or other lawful process, with notice where appropriate, minimization, auditability and remedies.

### Model constitutional amendment — discussion draft, not legal advice
**Section 1.** The people retain the right to access, obtain, understand, correct, export, secure and use personal information pertaining to themselves, subject to narrowly tailored laws protecting others' rights and legitimate public interests.

**Section 2.** Neither the United States nor any State shall deny or burden access to the internet, digital communications or a person's own personal information, nor impose civil or criminal punishment, solely because that person lawfully exercises a right protected by this article.

**Section 3.** Government acquisition, search, monitoring or compelled disclosure of private digital information shall comply with the Constitution, including applicable requirements of particularity, lawful process, proportionality, minimization and independent judicial review. Emergency exceptions must be narrow, time-limited and subject to prompt review.

**Section 4.** Congress and the States may enforce this article through appropriate legislation, including access, correction, portability, security, transparency, anti-retaliation, oversight and effective-remedy provisions.

**Section 5.** Nothing in this article immunizes unlawful conduct, obstructs a lawful investigation supported by independent evidence, or authorizes access to another person's information without consent or other lawful authority.

### Model federal Act — outline
1. Definitions, scope, covered entities and exclusions.
2. Individual access, copy, correction, deletion where appropriate, portability and appeal rights.
3. Strong authentication without unreasonable barriers; accessible, free request channels.
4. Data minimization, purpose limitation, retention limits, security and breach notification.
5. Government-held information, public records, law-enforcement access and due process.
6. Warrant/process standards, particularity, minimization, notice and delayed-notice review.
7. Anti-retaliation and anti-discrimination for lawful rights exercise.
8. Independent oversight, regulator enforcement, audit records and transparency reporting.
9. Effective remedies, civil penalties, carefully scoped private enforcement and whistleblower protection.
10. Federal/state interaction, severability, implementation and periodic review.
