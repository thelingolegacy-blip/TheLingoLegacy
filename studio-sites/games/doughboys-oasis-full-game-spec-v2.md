# DOUGHBOY'S OASIS — Full Game Blueprint v2
**Date:** 2026-10-13  
**Game ID:** DOUGHBOYS_OASIS  
**Status:** Expanded design and implementation contract. This document does not claim gameplay, assets, backend, audio, multiplayer, or release certification are complete.

## 1. Product promise
**DOUGHBOY'S OASIS — Your Life. Your World. Your Legacy.**  
Secondary line: **No one builds an empire alone.**  
Core catchphrase: **Read the room. Work the problem. Find your way out.**  
Player motto: **You don't have to know everything. You have to know what to do with what you know.**

A persistent open-world life-simulation and adventure game. Players can live an everyday life, work, build businesses, customize characters, explore, race, enter tournaments, solve problems, form crews, and uncover the Lazarus network linking distinct worlds. The game takes inspiration from the freedom and systemic density of major sandbox games while establishing its own story, tone, art, characters, and mechanics.

**Pillars**
1. Many intelligences: street smarts, book smarts, creativity, social intelligence, technical skill, leadership, observation, and resilience.
2. Multiple valid solutions: build, discover, inherit, improvise, negotiate, or stumble upon a solution.
3. Choices have consequences; no single moral stat determines whether a player is good or bad.
4. Failure teaches. The three-death Burial system causes a meaningful but recoverable setback.
5. The world is playable as a daily-life sim even if the player postpones the main story.
6. Accessible, fair progression; no pay-to-win and no paid advantage in ranked competitions.

## 2. Setting and historical-to-modern story engine
The opening district is **Amity Heights**, a fictionalized South Jersey city neighborhood inspired by the region's industrial towns, neighborhoods, shore corridors, farms, and road/rail links. Do not reproduce a private residence, real apartment unit, or identifiable real resident. Locations and people are fictional composites.

The narrative borrows broad social themes associated with the 1950s–1970s and translates them into present-day systems rather than reenacting real tragedies or using real residents as villains:
- Housing access, redlining legacies, displacement, rent pressure, and neighborhood reinvestment.
- Industrial closures, labor organizing, wage insecurity, automation, and transformation of local jobs.
- Unequal access to schools, libraries, technical education, and reliable information.
- Environmental contamination, waste, clean water, heat waves, flooding, and unequal infrastructure.
- Transportation gaps, long commutes, road safety, and access to work and services.
- Food affordability, grocery access, supply disruptions, and community gardens.
- Small-business credit, predatory contracts, monopolies, and barriers to ownership.
- Privacy, surveillance, misinformation, reputation systems, and algorithmic gatekeeping.
- Generational wealth gaps, family caregiving, community safety, and the tension between individual ambition and collective needs.

**Small problems:** a broken bus stop, a lost delivery, a power bill, a barber appointment, a closed corner store, a flooded basement, a missing library book, a school fundraiser, a broken refrigerator, or a neighbor who needs a ride.  
**Large problems:** a factory closure, a water-system failure, a heat emergency, a housing redevelopment dispute, a manipulated community-board election, a supply-chain crisis, or a powerful group attempting to control access to an essential service.

Missions present competing perspectives and evidence. Players can organize, investigate, negotiate, build, report, compete, or create alternatives. Avoid simplistic “one group is always evil” storytelling. Consequences should be visible at street level and over time.

## 3. Main character and supporting cast
**Doughboy** is the lead character and the player's primary avatar. The name is the character's identity, not a claim that every player must use one fixed appearance. Character creation supports body/face options, skin tones, hair, clothing, voice options, pronouns, mobility/accessibility settings, and a non-stereotyped range of personalities.

Proposed supporting roles (names are placeholders pending creator approval):
- **Mack “Switch” Bell:** street-smart connector; reads people and changing situations.
- **Nia Wells:** engineer and maker; turns scrap and overlooked materials into working systems.
- **Professor June Mercer:** community archivist; helps players separate records, rumor, and evidence.
- **Tee “Ledger” Brooks:** business-minded planner; understands contracts, budgets, and supply chains.
- **Rook Alvarez:** racer and route scout; knows roads, shortcuts, and vehicle handling.
- **Auntie Ren:** neighborhood organizer and oral-history keeper; remembers how policy decisions changed the district.
- **The Curator:** ambiguous figure associated with Lazarus; may help, mislead, or test the player depending on evidence and choices.

Characters should have goals, flaws, loyalties, routines, relationship arcs, and optional side stories. No supporting character is a walking tutorial only.

## 4. World and round design
The first five worlds spell G.R.E.E.D. Each represents a different system, not a claim that ordinary needs are inherently bad.

| Round / world | Meaning | Core challenge | Signature item | Escape / advancement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G — **Gold Rush** | Greed for Gold: wealth, ownership, opportunity | Create value with limited resources; inspect contracts and avoid traps | **Golden Ledger** | Earn the First Stake, expose a false ownership claim, discover the first Lazarus marker |
| R — **Resource Row** | Greed for Resources: supplies, land, energy, labor | Restore a disrupted supply chain while balancing cost, fairness, and speed | **Source Core** | Reconnect a power/water node or build a viable alternative route |
| E — **Elite Heights** | Greed for Exclusivity: status, access, influence | Gain entry through skill, service, evidence, relationship, or negotiation | **Crown Key** | Earn an invitation or expose an access system's hidden rules |
| E — **Echo Archives** | Greed for Education/Information: knowledge and secrecy | Reconstruct truth from conflicting records, codes, and witness accounts | **Memory Cube** | Recover a verified blueprint and learn Lazarus's activation logic |
| D — **Dominion District** | Greed for Dominion: control and power | Coordinate people and resources; decide how authority should be used | **Dominion Seal** | Complete a leadership trial and authorize the first gateway |
| 6 — **Obsession Maze** | The cost of chasing one outcome at any cost | Observe patterns, change approach, and recognize false progress | **Obsidian Compass** | Find a fair, clue-supported route out; no rescue shortcut |
| 7 — **Deception Grid** | Manipulation and manufactured certainty | Cross-check evidence, identify forged signals, and resist false choices | **Truth Lens** | Verify the real route through corroborated clues |
| 8 — **Scarcity Engine** | Artificial scarcity and distribution | Restore systems and allocate limited supplies transparently | **Balance Core** | Stabilize essential services and open the transit chamber |
| 9 — **Throne of Control** | Domination and accountability | Challenge a coercive system through teamwork, proof, engineering, or public support | **Sovereign Key** | Disable the control mechanism without destroying the community |
| 10 — **G.R.E.E.D. Nexus** | The combined system and the player's legacy | Decide how power, knowledge, resources, and access should be shared | **Lazarus Prime** | Open new worlds and choose what legacy to leave |

Later worlds are a roadmap, not a claim of completed content. Each world needs main missions, side missions, ambient events, collectible lore, accessibility routes, checkpoints, at least two viable solution paths for critical objectives, and a readable objective journal.

## 5. Lazarus and escape routes
Lazarus is a mysterious cross-world transport network, not just a single vehicle. It may manifest as a broken machine, gate, capsule, vehicle, lift, or unfamiliar structure depending on the world.

The first five items work together:
- Golden Ledger: reveals the ownership/access record.
- Source Core: powers the device.
- Crown Key: authorizes entry to restricted facilities.
- Memory Cube: reconstructs operating knowledge.
- Dominion Seal: grants activation authority.

Escape-route categories:
1. **Primary route:** clearly signposted and learnable.
2. **Skill route:** unlock through a tournament or demonstrated capability.
3. **Social route:** negotiate, build trust, or secure a community agreement.
4. **Engineering route:** repair or construct an alternative.
5. **Exploration route:** discover a hidden path or component.
6. **Shortcut route:** optional, earned through clues and mastery; never required to understand the main story.

No critical mission may create an irreversible dead end. Mark routes in the journal after discovery. Use spatial audio and visual signage, with text/caption alternatives. Environmental hazards are fictionalized and do not present as real-world emergency instructions.

## 6. Checkpoints, death, Burial, and recovery
**Three-death rule:** in a standard story challenge, the third counted death triggers **THE BURIAL** and returns the player one world for a refresher. First and second deaths respawn at the most recently activated checkpoint.

- First death: short contextual recap.
- Second death: optional hint or practice prompt.
- Third death: cinematic Burial, clear explanation, one-world setback, and a targeted refresher challenge.
- Tutorial deaths, confirmed bugs, disconnects, and server failures do not count.
- Warn before the third counted death; show the death counter and rules in the pause menu.
- Preserve permanent unlocks, achievements, discovered lore, character customization, completed world records, and major collectibles.
- Reset only clearly labeled temporary mission progress and unbanked temporary rewards.
- The refresher teaches a relevant skill and gives a recovery bonus, such as a clue, practice activity, or alternate route. It must not shame the player or demand a purchase.
- Optional relaxed mode can disable the world setback; ranked challenge rules remain explicit and consistent.

Checkpoint types: survival, skill, discovery, business, crew, world gate, and Lazarus. Autosaves must be transactional and resilient to disconnects. Players can see when a save succeeded.

## 7. Tournaments and progression
Tournament categories: Street Smarts, Book Smarts, Hustle, Engineering, Survival, Racing, Exploration, Leadership, Negotiation, and All-Around. Formats: solo, duo, four-player squad, crew, regional, and global seasonal events.

Scoring is published before entry. It may include objective completion, time, resource efficiency, evidence quality, teamwork, and style where relevant. Tie-breaks are deterministic and testable. No purchased boosts in ranked play. Top 10 / Top 5 / Top 3 status is seasonal or event-scoped; no player can permanently block core world progression.

Progression tracks are separate:
- **Skill mastery:** what the player can do.
- **World qualification:** story advancement.
- **Reputation:** how characters and groups respond.
- **Legacy:** durable contributions to people, places, and systems.
- **Crew progression:** cooperative achievements.
- **Cosmetics:** appearance and expression.

## 8. Radio and audio system
Radio is a diegetic in-world feature available in vehicles, at homes/businesses, and from a portable player if unlocked. UI provides station next/previous, direct station selection, favorite, volume, mute, audio ducking, subtitle/lyrics policy, and an option to turn off DJ chatter.

Proposed fictional stations:
- **Oasis AM — The Neighborhood Line:** local news, community bulletins, mission hints, interviews.
- **Goldline 88.5 — Soul & Classics:** original or properly licensed soul-inspired programming.
- **Jersey Static — Hip-Hop & Street Stories:** original beats, local fictional artists, freestyle segments.
- **Shoreline FM — R&B / Slow Jams:** relationship and nighttime driving mood.
- **Backroad Country — Country, Blues & Roots:** workday and rural/shore travel ambience.
- **Pulse Circuit — Electronic / Future Sound:** races, engineering, and high-tech worlds.
- **Archive 70 — Retro / Funk / Disco:** era-inspired sound without unlicensed recordings.
- **Lazarus Signal — Ambient / Experimental:** cryptic tones and story clues; unlockable.
- **Player Mix — Favorites:** player-curated licensed/owned tracks where platform rules permit.
- **Quiet Ride:** ambience only, no music or DJ.

Station identity can change by region, story state, weather, tournament, and player choices. DJ lines must be original and skippable. Music is streamed only from approved/owned/licensed assets; no scraping or unlicensed commercial tracks.

**Audio layers:** adaptive music stems, vehicle engine layers, tire/surface audio, footsteps by material, UI confirmations, item discovery, objective success/failure, Lazarus hum, environmental ambience, crowd response, NPC voice, radio transitions, and accessibility sound cues. Essential audio information also has visual and caption alternatives.

## 9. Sound and animation standards
Animation states: idle, walk, jog, sprint, crouch where appropriate, jump, climb, interact, carry, repair, negotiate, celebrate, stumble, damage reaction, respawn, enter/exit vehicle, radio controls, checkpoint activation, item discovery, Lazarus activation, Burial, and world transition.

Signature visual/audio beats:
- **The Read:** brief environmental emphasis, not a time-stopping superpower by default.
- **Discovery:** item glint plus a distinct three-note cue; captions announce meaningful sound.
- **Checkpoint:** a ring of light travels across the marker; soft mechanical chime; autosave indicator.
- **Burial:** brief non-graphic symbolic transition; lower mix, dust/ink motif, return-to-world card; no frightening imagery required.
- **Lazarus:** layered mechanical tones and spatial light paths that align as components activate.
- **Tournament finish:** clear rank/result animation with reduced-motion alternative.

Use motion-reduction settings, no rapid strobing, adjustable camera shake, captions, remappable controls, scalable text, contrast options, and audio sliders for music, effects, dialogue, radio, and ambience.

## 10. Logos, emblems, and mottos
Visual direction: original **industrial-noir meets South Jersey neighborhood warmth**—weathered enamel, street-map lines, brass/gold accents, deep teal, asphalt charcoal, cream, and a small signal-orange highlight. Avoid copying existing game logos or recognizable commercial marks.

- **Primary wordmark:** DOUGHBOY'S OASIS; a path/road forms an O and leads to a small oasis ring.
- **Game emblem:** an open circle crossed by a road line, with five small marks for G.R.E.E.D.
- **Lazarus emblem:** a broken ring with one reconnected segment; represents escape, repair, and second chances.
- **Tournament emblem:** a split shield with a road and puzzle-grid motif.
- **Crew emblem system:** player-generated shapes, colors, and symbols with moderation and trademark/impersonation safeguards.

Mottos:
- Main: “Your Life. Your World. Your Legacy.”
- Adventure: “Find the Lazarus. Build the Oasis. Decide What Happens Next.”
- Competition: “Every World Tests Something Different.”
- Recovery: “You Can Lose a Round Without Losing Your Future.”
- Community: “No One Builds an Empire Alone.”

Logos are design direction until original SVG/PNG masters, animation variants, and approval are created.

## 11. In-world Lingo and callouts
Initial fictional slang glossary; dialogue is contextual and must not stereotype real communities:
- **Read the room:** assess the situation before acting.
- **Catch the signal:** notice a clue or meaningful pattern.
- **Make a lane:** create a legitimate opportunity or route.
- **Hold the chain:** protect a team's or community's shared progress.
- **Bank it:** secure temporary earnings at a safe point.
- **Ghost route:** hidden optional path.
- **Cold ledger:** an unverified or disputed account.
- **Burned bridge:** a damaged relationship that requires repair.
- **Fresh start:** a post-Burial refresher.
- **Lazarus-ready:** all required activation conditions have been met.
- **Top-side:** current world or surface layer; exact meaning varies by world.
- **No free pass:** a shortcut still requires a legitimate condition.

NPCs can teach terms through context; the player can disable slang subtitles or consult an in-game glossary.

## 12. Cheat codes and sandbox controls
Cheats are an intentional, transparent feature for single-player experimentation and creator fun—not an exploit against other players.
- **OASISMODE:** enter a clearly labeled sandbox session with generous resources.
- **GHOSTROUTE:** reveal already-authored optional route markers in sandbox.
- **RADIOCHECK:** cycle all unlocked stations.
- **FRAMEIT:** enter photo mode.
- **REPLAYTHAT:** replay the last eligible story scene.
- **REFRESHME:** start the current world's practice refresher without a death penalty.
- **MAKERSPACE:** enable approved build/test tools in private creator mode.

Codes are proposed names and must be implemented and tested before being advertised as functional. Cheats are disabled in ranked tournaments, shared economy transactions, achievements requiring integrity, and competitive multiplayer. Activating a cheat flags the session as sandboxed; no score, wallet, or progression exploit is allowed. Platform-specific cheat input methods should be optional and accessible.

## 13. Payments, wallet, and store policy
**Payments must be distinct from gameplay skill and story qualification.** All prices and methods are shown before confirmation. No real-money wagering, cash-out, cash prizes, or redeemable gambling in this baseline.

Earnable, non-redeemable currencies:
- **Dough:** everyday earned currency for food, transit, basic clothing, repairs, and local services.
- **Legacy Tokens:** earned through story, community projects, discoveries, and verified achievements; used for selected cosmetics and keepsakes.
- **Crew Credits:** earned by shared objectives; spent only through role-authorized crew systems.
- **Event Tickets:** earned or granted for eligible events; used to enter non-cash tournaments.
- **Lazarus Shards:** rare exploration collectibles used to unlock lore and cosmetic device variants, not to buy competitive power.

Store rules:
- Core story, escape routes, checkpoints, and competitive capability cannot be paywalled.
- Optional purchases, if enabled in a later approved build, are limited to transparent cosmetics, expansions, or convenience features that do not create ranked advantage.
- Support platform-approved payment methods only (such as platform wallet or approved card/mobile payment options where supported); never store raw card data.
- Use platform billing requirements, age/guardian controls where required, receipts, refunds/support paths, spending confirmation, purchase history, and regional tax/currency disclosures.
- No direct player cash transfers, withdrawals, crypto, or unauthorized real-money trading.
- The baseline is free/demo-first with monetization disabled until legal, platform, security, and human approval gates pass.

## 14. Core UI and navigation
Title screen: Play, Continue, New Story, Daily Life, Multiplayer, Tournaments, Radio, Character, Crew, Map, Journal, Collection, Settings, Accessibility, Help, Credits, and Exit. Store is visible only if approved and enabled.

HUD: objective, compass/map toggle, health/status only where needed, current currency, checkpoint/save state, radio station, interaction prompt, world/death counter, and accessibility shortcuts. Avoid clutter; every HUD element can be remapped or selectively hidden.

Required screens: character creator; tutorial; pause; map; inventory; journal; skill/legacy page; world gate; checkpoint result; Burial/refresher; tournament lobby/result; radio station list; payment confirmation/history; crew permissions; report/block/mute; network status; save conflict; error/retry; credits and content settings.

## 15. Multiplayer and crews
Phase in friends, invites, parties, small co-op missions, racing, exploration, crew creation, shared objectives, HQ/workshop, permissioned shared assets, leaderboards, and regional events. Global persistence should use regional servers and instanced activities rather than one giant server. Solo remains complete and playable.

Roles may include Founder, Leader, Right Hand, Treasurer, Recruiter, Specialist, Member, and Prospect. Shared funds require transaction history, spending limits, explicit permissions, audit logs, and recovery paths. Use server-authoritative validation, anti-cheat, rate limits, reporting, blocking, moderation, appeal, privacy settings, and safe chat. Essential zones cannot be permanently monopolized by a single crew.

## 16. Technical architecture contract
Align with the existing Lingo Legacy stack only after inspecting current repository conventions:
- Client: prototype in the currently supported web/game framework; a full 3D console-grade experience may require Unity/Unreal and cannot be inferred from a website preview.
- Identity: Firebase Auth / Lingo ID only through approved integration.
- Persistence: Firestore or approved backend, with schema versioning and server-validated writes.
- Economy/progression: Cloud Functions or trusted server APIs; never trust client wallet/score claims.
- Content: versioned manifests for world definitions, dialogue, assets, stations, items, and mission graphs.
- Audio: asset IDs, cue metadata, rights/licensing, locale, loudness, captions, and fallbacks.
- Telemetry: privacy-minimized events, consent and retention controls, no secrets or precise location.
- Feature flags: story worlds, radio, tournaments, payments, multiplayer, and creator tools independently disableable.
- CI: lint, typecheck, unit tests, schema checks, accessibility checks, content/asset license checks, integration tests, E2E smoke tests, security review, independent verification, and rollback evidence.

Suggested domain modules: worlds, missions, character, inventory, economy, checkpoints, burial, tournaments, radio, media, lazarus, crew, moderation, telemetry, and platform-adapters.

## 17. Implementation phases and acceptance gates
1. **Playable vertical slice:** title/menu, character selection, one Amity Heights block, movement/interactions, one job, inventory, one mission, checkpoint/save, radio toggle with placeholder/owned audio, one escape puzzle.
2. **World systems:** Gold Rush and Resource Row, resources, stores, contracts, day/night/weather, NPC schedules, core sound/animation, first Lazarus item.
3. **G.R.E.E.D. campaign:** all five worlds, item chain, mission branching, alternative routes, world gate, story journal, Death/Burial/refresher.
4. **Activities:** tournaments, racing, daily-life sandbox, cheat/sandbox controls, collectibles, accessibility and localization.
5. **Persistent services:** account sync, server-validated economy/progression, leaderboards, moderation, privacy/export/delete.
6. **Multiplayer/crews:** small sessions first; shared objectives and permissions before large events.
7. **Content expansion:** worlds 6–10, global seasons, creator worlds, additional platform adapters, immersive/VR only after comfort and input tests.

**Release gates:** source authority → build reproducibility → automated tests → security/privacy → accessibility/content review → asset rights → device/platform QA → independent live validation → approval. A blocked CI runner or missing evidence blocks promotion; do not merge or claim production readiness solely because a design document exists.

## 18. Minimum vertical-slice acceptance checklist
- Player can start, pause, resume, restart, save, load, and exit without corrupting progress.
- One neighborhood can be explored with working collision, interaction prompts, at least one NPC, one job, one store, and one meaningful choice.
- Radio can be toggled, stations switched, volume adjusted, and muted; captions/visual alternatives exist for essential cues.
- One mission has two valid solution paths and a visible checkpoint.
- Death counter correctly ignores tutorial and verified technical failures; third counted death triggers Burial and the intended refresher.
- One tournament uses published scoring and deterministic tie-breaks.
- Cheat codes work only in sandbox and cannot alter ranked or shared-economy state.
- Payment UI is disabled in the baseline, or if enabled later, uses approved platform billing and never offers wagering/cash-out.
- Accessibility, audio rights, and all asset licenses have documented sign-off.
- CI/test evidence, preview URL, device QA, and rollback path are independently verified before any production promotion.
