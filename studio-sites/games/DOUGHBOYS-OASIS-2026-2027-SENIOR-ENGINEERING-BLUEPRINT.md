# DOUGHBOY'S OASIS — Senior Engineering & Game Experience Blueprint 2026–2027

**Branch:** `studio/doughboys-oasis-full-game-2026-10-13`  
**Status:** implementation contract plus local interactive prototype. No production, platform certification, or full 3D completion is implied.

## North-star experience
**The grind. The hustle. The plan. The scheme. The upcome. The makings of a true Oasis master. GREED IS JUST THE BEGINNING. WAIT UNTIL…**

Doughboy has mastered surviving the Oasis. He is a mystery, leader and boss; observant, calculated, a family man, popular by default. His influence comes from participation and example, not speeches alone. The world sees him take a hard shift, check the details, clean up what he damages, protect his people, and accept consequences. His mystique comes from what the player observes him do—not from a blank check to excuse harm.

Core lines:
- “He don't just talk about it. He be about it.”
- “Read the room. Work the problem.”
- “Get in, get out, don’t take too much.”
- “Clean up where you mess up.”
- “Fear is not respect. Trust is built.”
- “Secure the basics, then build the dream.”
- “If you don’t know, ask. If you know, show.”
- “Move calculated. Leave people better.”
- “Sink or Rise.”

## Experience pillars
1. **Cinematic, authored world:** high-quality character performance, facial animation, authored camera language, lighting/weather/time-of-day, layered city ambience, reactive interiors, radio, licensed/original soundtrack and seamless transitions. Browser prototype is a system demonstrator; final 3D production requires a chosen engine, art pipeline and device budgets.
2. **Life simulation:** routines, sleep, meals, hygiene, cleaning, work, education, transport, rent/bills, maintenance, family, recreation and rest.
3. **Player agency:** negotiate, plan, build, ask for help, learn, improvise, walk away or accept consequences. No single moral score reduces a player to “good” or “bad.”
4. **Life-stage learning:** Kid, Teen and Adult are age-appropriate narrative lenses with different options, responsibilities, support systems and consequences. No humiliating failure loops; explain why a choice matters.
5. **World-building:** room repair → home expansion → business → block project → community spaces → district investments. Building has material cost, time, accessibility, maintenance, permits/permissions where appropriate, NPC jobs and neighborhood consequences.
6. **Persistent story memory:** meaningful player decisions, promises, betrayals, lessons, repairs, relationships, sacrifices and rival responses are saved as visible, inspectable game events. No covert real-world surveillance.
7. **Family and care:** an optional newborn/family arc can teach preparation, shared responsibility, time budgeting, support networks and care routines. Do not lock parentage, co-parent identity, or whose baby it is until the creator approves canon. Do not gamify real medical advice as authoritative.
8. **Humor with heart:** quick banter, wordplay, absurd neighborhood situations, memorable NPC routines, funny failures, radio callers, and warm family moments without trivializing hardship.
9. **Daily return by value, not compulsion:** fresh optional neighborhood stories, rotating jobs, skill challenges, build progress, family moments and learning prompts. No punitive streak loss, deceptive timers, manipulative notifications, or pay-to-win.

## Life-stage learning contract
### Kid — Learn the Basics
- Tasks: tidy a room, return borrowed items, plan playtime, save toward a goal, ask an adult for help, understand feelings and consequences.
- Concepts: needs vs. wants, time, safety, kindness, simple money arithmetic, repairing mistakes.
- Failure: low-stakes, short explanation, retry and encouragement. No rent debt, predatory contracts, or adult criminal responsibilities.

### Teen — Find Your Lane
- Tasks: school/work balance, first job shifts, transport planning, peer pressure, consent/boundaries, online misinformation, saving, job applications, teamwork.
- Concepts: hourly pay, schedules, opportunity cost, scams, privacy, reliable sources, conflict de-escalation.
- Failure: missed opportunity and recoverable setback; trusted adult/mentor routes remain available.

### Adult — Carry the Load
- Tasks: rent and bills calendar, income variability, household upkeep, food and transport budgets, career development, business management, family care, emergencies and community participation.
- Concepts: due dates, prioritization, total cost of ownership, contracts, emergency funds, asking for a payment plan, seeking legitimate local assistance, balancing needs and wants.
- Avoid moralizing poverty. A player can make a good plan and still face an unfair system or an unexpected hardship.

Stage-specific content should be configurable, accessibility-reviewed, and age-appropriate. Player-selected life stage must not be treated as verified real-world age or used for sensitive ad targeting.

## Core daily loop
1. **Observe:** inspect calendar, weather, NPC mood, home condition, obligations and opportunities.
2. **Prioritize:** choose a need, commitment, skill, relationship, or want.
3. **Act:** work, learn, clean, cook, repair, negotiate, rest, race, explore, or help.
4. **Resolve:** time, energy, cash, trust, home condition and NPC reactions update.
5. **Reflect:** askLINGO explains the lesson; player can challenge the advice, ask for another perspective, or ignore it.
6. **Remember:** a story event records the decision and meaningful consequence.
7. **Plan tomorrow:** optional next step and fresh opportunity; no penalty for leaving the game.

## Economy and home systems
- Demo wallet remains virtual and non-cashable.
- Needs (food, safety, rest, essential transport) are separated from wants (luxury outfits, entertainment, status items).
- Rent/bills include due dates, reminders, budgeting, landlord/provider conversations, payment-plan branches, roommate/shared-responsibility options and community support. Fictional bill screens never contact real providers.
- Cleaning/maintenance: mess sources, room condition, tools, time cost, shared chores, repair-vs-replace decisions and consequence prevention.
- Jobs: age-appropriate role availability, skill requirements, schedules, wage transparency, workplace safety, colleague relationships, promotion paths and fair exit choices.
- Business: inventory, customer trust, costs, cash flow, hiring, maintenance, community impact and ethical tradeoffs.
- Buildings: modular rooms, furniture, style, access, safety, utilities, repair states, storage, workshop/shop, public-space upgrades and neighborhood effects. Actual 3D placement is future implementation.
- Hardship is systemic as well as personal: factory closures, rent pressure, water/heat/flooding, transit gaps, misinformation and predatory contracts are represented through fictional composites and multiple solutions.

## Newborn / family arc (optional and canon-pending)
A new baby can be Doughboy's child or belong to another character, but this remains unconfirmed canon. Do not infer parentage from the feature request. Narrative mechanics can include:
- preparing a safe home, supplies and a practical care schedule;
- asking a trusted partner/family/community member to share duties;
- balancing work shifts and family time;
- protecting rest and relationships;
- appointments and reliable information represented in non-diagnostic terms;
- choices that change trust, support and later story opportunities.
The game should not reward neglect or punish players with graphic content. It should be warm, humorous in appropriate moments, and honest about exhaustion and support.

## Original “Oasis Decks” system
Build original visual card decks with a signature art direction; do not reproduce Scarface characters, logos, cards, UI, dialogue or recognizable assets. “Scarface decks” is interpreted as a request for bold crime-drama strategy-card energy, not licensed or copied content.
- **Boss Deck:** lead by doing; crew morale and trust.
- **Observer Deck:** inspect contracts, verify rumors, notice contradictions.
- **Survival Deck:** needs, bills, repairs and contingency planning.
- **Wildcard Deck:** humor, improvisation and creative alternatives.
- **Builder Deck:** materials, zoning/permissions, maintenance and neighborhood impact.
- **Family Deck:** commitments, care, shared responsibility and relationship repair.
Cards should unlock through play/learning and provide situational options—not purchased competitive power. A card can surface tradeoffs, not erase consequences.

## LINGO moves and assistants
- **askLINGO:** plain-language explainer, asks clarifying questions, breaks complex situations into manageable choices.
- **LINGOai:** compare options, show assumptions, identify risk and uncertainty, offer more than one path. It must not pretend to be a qualified legal, medical or financial professional.
- **LINGOboom:** quick challenge/quiz, wordplay, practical mini-missions and recall prompts.
- **LINGOlibrary:** optional glossary and learning archive.
- **LINGO memory:** save only meaningful in-game decisions; player can inspect, export, reset and eventually delete synced history.
All three assistants in the current Life Lab are local prepared-response prototypes, not live AI connections. Production AI requires backend mediation, privacy controls, rate limits, content safety, cost controls and tests.

## Technical architecture targets
- **Client:** cross-platform presentation layer; input abstraction; accessible UI; offline-friendly save queue; versioned save schema.
- **Game simulation:** deterministic state transitions for jobs, bills, needs, building, relationship events and race rules; seeded simulation for reproducible tests.
- **Identity:** authenticated player profile, opt-in sync and safe recovery. No sensitive tokens in localStorage or bundled assets.
- **Backend:** authoritative wallets, inventory, partner/relationship mutations, competitive race results and shared building state. Validate identity, authorization, schemas, idempotency and rate limits.
- **Story event model:** event_id, schema_version, save_id, in_game_day, actor, event_type, choice_id, context tags, consequences, related characters, visibility and created_at. Store only necessary fictional game state.
- **Observability:** correlation IDs, structured logs, error boundaries, crash/performance telemetry with privacy minimization. Client telemetry is not release evidence.
- **Security:** secret scanning, dependency audit, CSP, input validation, authorization tests, backup/recovery tests, abuse/race replay protection.
- **Content pipeline:** authored narrative data separate from engine code; locale keys; VO transcript/subtitle files; rights metadata for every art/audio/font asset; age-stage tags and content review.
- **Build pipeline:** source validation → unit tests → accessibility/static analysis → asset/license manifest → client builds → device runtime QA → security/privacy review → certification evidence → human authorization. Fail closed at each gate.

## Cinematic art and audio direction
Industrial noir with gold and royal-purple accents; rich shadows and readable silhouettes; lived-in neighborhood props; expressive animation; distinct visual language per district; grounded materials and weather; humorous character timing; original radio identity and score. Prioritize stable frame pacing, dynamic resolution and accessibility over visual effects that obscure gameplay. Build an asset budget per target (mobile, web, TV, PC/console) before committing to photorealistic features.

## 2026–2027 staged roadmap
- **Wave 0 — Foundation:** preserve fail-closed release state; approve canon; define engine/platform targets, feature IDs, save schema, event taxonomy and acceptance tests.
- **Wave 1 — Life Loop:** needs/energy, cleaning, work/skill actions, bills planner, local save migration, learning feedback.
- **Wave 2 — Home & Building:** modular build data, cost/upkeep, first room/shop/block projects, visual placement prototype.
- **Wave 3 — Story & Family:** relationship memory, optional newborn arc after canon approval, life-stage content validation.
- **Wave 4 — Race & Decks:** input abstraction, deterministic race prototype, original decks, no wagering.
- **Wave 5 — Cinematic vertical slice:** selected engine, one polished district, authored story mission, facial/character animation, sound and performance budget.
- **Wave 6 — Cross-platform pilot:** web + one native target first; LG webOS after remote UX prototype; device acceptance matrix.
- **Wave 7 — Online services & launch readiness:** server-authoritative sync/leaderboards, privacy/security tests, cost controls, independent CI and platform certification.
Every wave needs explicit acceptance criteria and evidence; do not label planned work as implemented.

## Measurable acceptance criteria
- Save/load/migration preserves stage, economy, buildings, partner/family state and event history.
- Need/want, rent/bill and job choices produce explainable outcomes and at least two viable solutions.
- Age-stage content filters are tested for inappropriate option leakage.
- Player can inspect/export/reset local story memory; no covert device sensors.
- Building costs, home state and upkeep are deterministic and testable.
- askLINGO/LINGOai/LINGOboom disclose whether responses are local or live; no secret-bearing client configuration.
- Keyboard, touch, controller and TV-remote focus flows are tested on actual target devices.
- Performance targets are selected per platform before visual fidelity is locked.
- G02, CI, security, runtime QA, privacy, asset rights, certification and human approval must pass before production promotion.

4. What is the chosen production engine for cinematic 3D (Unity, Unreal, or another), and which first shipping target should be certified? Recommended default: one high-quality web/PC vertical slice before native expansion.
